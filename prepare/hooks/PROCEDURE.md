# Preparing hooks for a nonfiction unit

Stage 6 of the preparation pipeline (`../PLAN.md`), done by hand or by a model following [`prompt.md`](prompt.md) until it's automated. The rules for hooks live in `method/hooks.md`; this is the procedure for applying them ahead of time.

## Input

A spine unit with at least its claims and general ideas (`formats/spine.md`). Judgment targets, when the unit has them, are better still.

## Steps

1. **Pick the target.** Choose one judgment target: a question the learner should reach themselves. Apply the lookup test: if looking things up would settle it, it's information, not a target. Write it in plain words, without the book's vocabulary.
2. **Name the claim type.** Is the target about a cause, a test or framework, a recommendation, a prediction, a turning point, a distinction, or the author's credibility? Use the table in `method/hooks.md` ("Hooks for nonfiction") to pick candidate formats.
3. **Draft three hooks in different formats.** At least one should be personal (the learner's own work or life) when the learner context allows it. Use a scene when the target is about what to *do*.
4. **Gate each one** against the quality bar in `method/hooks.md`: truthful, paid off this session, felt before told, stakes, small, interesting without the source, not a lookup, fresh, personal where possible. Scenes also need the seven scene rules. Write a one-line verdict for each candidate, including why the rejected ones failed.
5. **Keep one or two.** Prefer different formats across a unit's hooks, so sessions don't repeat.
6. **Write them to the spine** under the unit's `hooks`, with `target`, and for scenes the `scene` block. Record rejected candidates and their reasons in a trial note (see [`trials/`](trials/)), not in the spine.

## Checks that catch most bad hooks

- **The headline makes a claim you can't defend.** Rewrite it or drop it. Catchy is fine; false is not.
- **The activity can be answered without thinking.** If the learner could answer in five seconds with no reasoning, there's nothing to debrief.
- **The debrief angle names the book's conclusion.** It should name what to *surface from the learner*, not what to tell them.
- **A scene where every choice works out.** Write the consequences so at least one tempting option goes wrong.
- **A role that's really the author.** Make it "someone who holds the author's view."
